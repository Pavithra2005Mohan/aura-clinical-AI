Viva Voce Defense Strategies & Rapid Question Formulation

Q1: Why fine-tune Bio_ClinicalBERT instead of prompting larger LLMs like LLaMA-3 or GPT-4?

General large language models introduce unacceptable inference latencies (often 1.5 to 3.0 seconds per record), demand massive cluster memory footprints, and pose hallucination hazards. Furthermore, transmitting protected health information (PHI) over external APIs violates strict hospital data residency standards. In contrast, Bio_ClinicalBERT fine-tuned with parametric attention pooling executes deterministically in 38.4 ms directly on hospital edge accelerators in complete compliance with HIPAA Safe Harbor regulations.

Q2: How does your architecture mathematically handle long clinical narratives exceeding 512 tokens?

Standard transformers enforce an $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ self-attention cost, truncating text at token 512 and discarding critical diagnostic conclusions or discharge prescriptions at the bottom of the document. We implemented overlapping sliding-window chunking ($L=512$, $S=384$, with a 128-token boundary overlap) coupled with parametric chunk attention pooling: $\alpha_k = \text{softmax}(v^T \tanh(W c_k + b))$. This allows the network to dynamically assign higher weights to salient chunks anywhere in the document, forming a single 768-dimensional document embedding vector without any data loss.

Q3: Why is Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) essential rather than solely relying on dense vector cosine search?

Dense semantic vector embeddings excel at concept matching (e.g., equating 'dyspnea' with 'shortness of breath'), but frequently lose precision on exact alphanumeric tokens such as specific medication dosages ('12.5 mg' vs '25 mg') or Patient Accession MRNs. Inverted BM25 keyword matching excels at exact identifiers but misses clinical synonyms. RRF harmonizes both lists through rank-reciprocal summation: $\text{RRF}(d) = \frac{1}{60 + r_{BM25}(d)} + \frac{1}{60 + r_{Dense}(d)}$, guaranteeing robustness across both exact dosage matching and synonym retrieval.

Q4: How does your Store D4 active retraining loop avoid catastrophic forgetting?

When clinician overrides are dispatched to Store D4, the model is not retrained from scratch. The flagged instances are treated as high-priority hard negatives and subjected to low-learning-rate updates ($\eta = 10^{-5}$) using AdamW with weight decay ($\lambda = 0.01$). Crucially, the batch is augmented with a permanent rehearsal buffer containing verified gold-standard samples from the original MTSamples/MIMIC distribution, preventing the parameter representation from drifting or forgetting prior clinical patterns.
